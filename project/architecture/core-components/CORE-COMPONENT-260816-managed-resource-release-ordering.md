# CORE-COMPONENT-260816-managed-resource-release-ordering: Managed Resource Release Ordering and Identity Retirement

## Status

Adopted

## Purpose

Ascend holds two kinds of state for one project: a durable registration row that survives process restarts, and volatile in-memory ownership of host resources — a workbench process, its owned process group, its loopback listener, its proxy connections, its pending admissions, and its quarantined identities. Any operation that removes the durable registration therefore destroys the only durable subject against which the volatile resources can later be accounted for.

If the durable removal happens first, or happens without exclusivity, the system can reach a state it can never repair from local evidence: a live Ascend-owned runtime with no registration to attribute it to, no public row to report it in, and no reconciliation subject to adopt or release it on the next API start. This component fixes the ordering, the exclusivity, and the retirement rules that make that state unreachable, so that every operation which removes durable state proves the volatile state is gone first. It is cross-cutting because the ordering binds the persistence boundary, the lifecycle boundary, the proxy boundary, the reconciliation boundary, and the evidence contract at once, and because every future durable-removal operation inherits the same obligation.

## Scope

Applies to every operation that removes a durable registration for a subject whose volatile resources Ascend owns, and to every boundary that participates in such an operation: the runtime-management boundary that owns entries, ownership records, admissions, quarantines, and cleanup audits; the connection boundary that owns per-subject transport resources; the persistence boundary that owns the durable row; and the recovery boundary that enumerates durable subjects after a process restart.

Out of scope: which resources a subject owns, how a resource is terminated, how a resource is attributed to a subject, how a subject's public state is projected, and the durable schema itself. Those are decided by the termination sequencer, host attribution evidence, public state projection, and persistence lifecycle components respectively; this component only orders their use and states what may be reported when.

## Definition

### Rules

- A durable registration removal MUST be preceded, in the same operation, by a positive confirmation that every Ascend-owned volatile resource for that subject is absent or exactly released. Removal MUST NOT be attempted concurrently with, before, or independently of that confirmation.
- Confirmation MUST be a **re-observation taken immediately before removal**, not a recollection of an earlier phase. The connection boundary's per-subject counts MUST be read again through a synchronous audit callable, and the whole final confirmation MUST be one uninterrupted synchronous region whose last statement is the durable removal, with no `await` between the last observation and that call. If the re-observation is non-zero the operation MAY re-run the per-subject release **at most once**, under the single already-declared release allowance, and MUST then re-observe; if it is still non-zero the operation MUST settle as an unconfirmed release rather than remove. An operation that confirms against a value observed before any suspension point can be defeated by a resource acquired during that suspension, which is a correctness defect and not a timing preference.
- Confirmation MUST be positive and total. It MUST require that no ownership record, no pending admission, no quarantined identity, and no unconfirmed cleanup result remains for the subject; that every generation released by the operation produced a release observation confirming the root process absent, its owned process group absent, and its loopback listener absent; and that the connection boundary reports zero pending operations, zero upstream requests, zero upstream responses, zero raw sockets, and zero bridged connections for that subject. Absence MUST NOT be inferred from a state label, from an entry's absence, from a prior recorded audit that did not itself confirm absence, or from the fact that no signal was needed.
- A subject with no owned volatile resources satisfies confirmation without any signal being sent. The operation MUST NOT launch, signal, probe, adopt, or terminate anything in that case, and MUST NOT convert "nothing to release" into "release performed".
- The operation MUST hold one exclusive claim on the subject that spans confirmation and removal, installed by one synchronous compare-and-set with no `await` between reading the subject's installed state and installing the claim. While the claim is installed, every other path that could install, revive, replace, or hand out a volatile resource for that subject MUST refuse with its own bounded, named, non-success outcome, and MUST NOT wait indefinitely, mutate the subject, or proceed on the assumption that the claim will fail.
- Every path that could hand out or install a volatile resource MUST re-evaluate the claim **after every `await`**, and MUST refuse before it installs an entry, revives an ownership record, or returns an existing resource handle to its caller. A single refusal evaluated at the head of such a path is insufficient, because the claim may be installed while that path is suspended; where the boundary already re-checks ownership after those same suspension points, it MUST re-check the claim at the same points.
- A path refused by the claim or the retirement set **before** it produced identity-bearing state — before it installed volatile state, registered or revived an ownership record, created a pending admission, minted a generation, or returned an existing resource handle — MUST be treated as a **refused acquisition**: bounded, named, mutating nothing, counted only for evidence, and MUST NOT be treated as outstanding work that blocks confirmation. Only work that **attempted or created identity-bearing state** for the subject while its claim was held — a refused volatile-state install, an ownership registration observed under a claim or after retirement, and an accounted stale settlement — is late work, and only that work MUST fail the confirmation closed. Treating a refused acquisition as outstanding work is prohibited: it makes the operation unconfirmable exactly when its own exclusivity mechanism is exercised, and the residue it appears to cover is already carried by the connection re-observation and the reference-identity clause.
- **Amended 2026-08-16 (Plan revision 6). A refused acquisition is not residue-free at the connection boundary, and the proof of the re-observation MUST be built from that fact.** A participating connection boundary registers its per-subject operation before it requests the acquisition it needs and releases that registration only when the operation unwinds. An arrival that reaches the boundary after the release completed therefore holds an audited per-subject resource for the whole time the claim's refusal takes to unwind, even though the refusal guarantees it never opens a new upstream connection. Two obligations follow. The claiming boundary MUST re-observe before removal on the **ordinary** path, not only against a defective one, and MUST NOT treat "the claim refused it" as "it left nothing behind". And a scenario proving that re-observation MUST be constructed from this ordering — an arrival after the completed release, held at one declared suspension point of the acquisition path, refused at its mandatory post-suspension claim re-evaluation — rather than from an acquisition that escapes the claim and opens an upstream connection, which the registration ordering makes unreachable. Where an invariant needs a live resource the single permitted re-release cannot clear, that resource MUST be produced by holding such an arrival past the declared release allowance, never by injecting a call the production path would not make.
- **Amended 2026-08-16 (Plan revision 7). One arrival cannot carry both halves of that proof, so the proof MUST be split.** An arrival suspended at a post-suspension claim re-evaluation necessarily entered the acquisition path *before* the claim existed, so the first release cannot settle until it unwinds and it can never still be registered at the final re-observation; an arrival that reaches the boundary *after* the first release settled is refused at the acquisition path's head, before any suspension point, so it never reaches a post-suspension re-evaluation at all. A proof that needs both the refused post-suspension acquisition and the still-registered resource MUST therefore use **two arrivals with declared, disjoint roles** — one admitted before the claim to witness the re-evaluation, one arriving after the first release to be the resource the re-observation sees — each suspended at a declared production seam, and each recorded with the settlement its own mechanism produced. Coupling the two roles onto one arrival is prohibited: it is unreachable, and every attempt to reach it ends in an injected call, a bypassed registration, or a relaxed rule.
- The boundary MUST hold **one** write authority over its volatile-state map. Exactly one lexical site may perform the map mutation, that site MUST consult the retirement set and the claim map before writing, and it MUST return its refusal to its caller; every other transition MUST call it, and a refused caller MUST NOT emit, register ownership, record cleanup, register a task, or report success. An install that cannot be refused by construction MUST assert success with its own path's invariant error rather than discard the result. The claim MUST additionally carry a **seal** set inside the confirmation region: while the seal is set, the write authority MUST refuse every caller including the claim owner, and an ownership registration for the subject MUST NOT enter the ownership index but MUST be recorded as a quarantined identity so the boundary shutdown sweep still reaches it. The seal MUST be lifted only by the operation, only on the failed-removal branch, and only immediately before it installs its released state; a completed removal MUST leave it superseded by retirement.
- A second request for the same subject while a claim is installed MUST join the claim's settlement rather than opening a second operation. It MUST perform no release, no removal, and no mutation of its own; it MUST derive its own outcome from the durable fact the winner established; and it MUST NOT report the winner's success as its own. Exactly one request per completed removal may report success and may emit the completion event.
- Where the operation sweeps a set of ownership records rather than a single resource, its release segment MUST be **cardinality-aware**: the boundary MUST freeze the subject's ownership cardinality inside the same synchronous section that installs the claim, MUST clamp that cardinality to a declared positive-safe-integer cap, and MUST derive the release segment as the clamped cardinality multiplied by the boundary's declared single-release bound. A frozen cardinality above the cap MUST be refused immediately with its own bounded named non-success that releases nothing, signals nothing, removes nothing, emits nothing, and mutates nothing. Multiplying a declared bound by an unbounded in-memory count is prohibited: a bound that cannot be stated before execution is not a bound, and a bound that a correct execution can exceed is not one either.
- The whole operation MUST settle within one declared finite bound that is fixed and documented before execution, composed of named segments whose sum is asserted in code. Every deadline on the operation's path, including deadlines that bound work performed by another boundary, MUST be armed from one trusted synchronous scheduling primitive on one monotonic clock owned by the claiming boundary. A participating boundary MUST NOT self-time work performed on behalf of this operation, and no fallible awaited delay primitive may pace any phase.
- **Amended 2026-08-16 (Plan revision 5).** "MUST NOT self-time" prohibits **authority over time**, not the use of a scheduling primitive. A participating boundary MUST NOT declare, derive, inherit, or infer a deadline; MUST NOT read a clock or measure elapsed time; MUST NOT count attempts, retries, or budgets; and MUST NOT let any of those decide whether its work succeeded, when it ended, or what it returned. It MAY pace its own **re-observation** of a settlement predicate it does not define, through one **non-authoritative observation gap**: a single fixed delay, stated as a literal in its source, that decides only when that predicate is read again. The gap MUST NOT terminate the wait, MUST NOT extend it beyond the caller's cancellation signal, MUST NOT be read, compared, accumulated, or counted, and MUST NOT reach any returned value or evidence field. The boundary MUST settle at most once; MUST cancel the gap's scheduled handle and remove its cancellation listener on that settlement path; and MUST leave no timer, listener, or callback able to observe or mutate its resources after it settles, with a callback delivered after settlement changing nothing. Where the boundary's own resources already publish an aggregate settlement event it MUST prefer that event to a gap; where they do not, it MUST NOT be required to invent one, because a notification that can be missed converts an ordinary release into an unconfirmed one and is a weaker guarantee than re-reading live state.
- A non-success MUST retain the durable registration exactly once, with every durable field unchanged, and MUST leave the subject in an explicit truthful state that a later action can act on. It MUST retain every ownership record it could not confirm released, so the boundary's own shutdown sweep re-attempts exact cleanup. It MUST NOT report completion, MUST NOT emit the completion event, and MUST NOT leave the subject in a transient claim state after settlement.
- A confirmed release followed by a failed durable removal MUST report a non-success, MUST leave the registration present exactly once, and MUST record the confirmed release so the subject reports its released state rather than its pre-release state. It MUST NOT recreate, relaunch, or signal a resource in order to restore consistency.
- A completed removal MUST retire the subject's identity: the boundary MUST delete its volatile entry and recorded outcomes and MUST record the identity in a retirement set. Every path that installs or revives volatile state MUST consult that set first and refuse. A retirement set entry MUST NOT expire while the process lives, and the identity MUST NOT be reissued by the durable boundary.
- Delayed, reordered, cancelled, or abandoned work from any earlier generation of a retired or claimed subject MUST settle with an explicit bounded stale, cancelled, or non-success outcome. It MUST NOT report success, recreate the durable registration, install or route to a resource for the subject, mutate the subject's state, emit a completion or success event, or affect any other subject.
- An interruption of the operating process MUST leave exactly one of two states: the registration present with an explicit truthful outcome recoverable by the recovery boundary, or the registration absent with every resource for that subject already positively confirmed absent before the removal committed. If a surviving candidate cannot be positively excluded or exactly attributed, the registration MUST remain present in an explicit unresolved or failed state. An absent registration with any surviving candidate is prohibited, and no boundary may report a state that implies it.
- **Amended 2026-08-16 (Plan revision 7). The recovered state is the recovery boundary's to settle, and the interruption's invariants are the ones stated above and no more.** Where a survivor **can** be exactly attributed, the recovery boundary MUST settle it through its own unchanged contract — including adopting it, where that contract adopts — and neither this component nor any proof of an interruption may require a non-recovery outcome for a survivor whose conditions hold. The invariants an interruption proof may assert unconditionally are: no durable registration was falsely removed; no signal was delivered by the interrupted operation to a resource it had not confirmed; the registration is enumerable by the recovery boundary; and the recovery result is truthful about what was observed. The recovered state itself MUST be stated as the recovery contract's own conditioned disjunction, with the branch and its deciding condition recorded. A count that more than one phase of such an episode can produce — signals above all — MUST be attributed to the phase that produced it: the interrupted operation, the transition to the replacement boundary, the recovery itself, and any later retry are separate accounts, and one aggregate claim over the whole episode is prohibited.
- The operation MUST NOT create, modify, move, delete, or read the contents of any directory the subject merely refers to. Removing a registration is not deleting the thing it registers, and no path in the operation may be given a filesystem write capability over that directory.
- The operation MUST persist nothing beyond the durable removal itself. Claims, retirement entries, ownership records, release markers, admissions, and volatile state MUST remain in memory, and the operation MUST NOT introduce a schema change or a migration.
- The operation MUST be announced only by its own completion event, emitted exactly once for a completed removal. It MUST NOT emit, synthesize, or reuse the success event of a neighbouring operation whose primitives it consumed. It MAY emit a truthful state-change event that it genuinely caused, and MUST classify it with the operation's own bounded category.
- Evidence for the operation MUST distinguish a completed zero-residual observation from an unresolved claim, MUST be produced by executing the production paths rather than by asserting expected values, and MUST record its residual audit as an independent re-observation taken after capture. A residual count MUST be an integer zero only when a completed probe observed absence, and MUST otherwise be an explicit withheld claim.

### Interfaces

- The claiming boundary MUST expose the operation as one member returning a frozen typed outcome with a closed vocabulary: one completion outcome, one already-absent outcome, and one rejection carrying exactly one bounded category. It MUST resolve every classified outcome as a value and MUST reject only on an infrastructure fault.
- The operation MUST receive the effects it does not own as injected callables rather than acquiring references to the boundaries that own them, so no construction cycle is created and the claiming boundary keeps sole ownership of ordering, bounds, and settlement.
- The claiming boundary MUST receive a **synchronous** per-subject re-observation callable in addition to the per-subject release callable, and MUST use it as the final confirmation input. An asynchronous re-observation is not acceptable, because awaiting it reopens the window it exists to close.
- The connection boundary MUST expose a per-subject release member that acts only on that subject's resources, is bounded solely by a caller-supplied cancellation signal, does not enter a global shutdown mode, does not resolve targets, does not start resources, and returns the per-subject audit the caller must find zero. **Amended 2026-08-16 (Plan revision 5):** it MUST terminate on exactly two conditions — its subject's own counts observed zero, or the caller's signal aborting — MAY re-read those counts across the non-authoritative observation gap defined above, MUST own no deadline, and MUST leave no scheduled handle and no cancellation listener alive once it returns.
- The persistence boundary MUST expose durable removal as one serialized transactional member whose result vocabulary distinguishes a removal from an already-absent subject, and MUST NOT expose a partial or best-effort removal.

### Expectations

- Consumers MUST treat "the registration is gone" as strictly stronger than "the resources are gone", never the reverse.
- Consumers MUST NOT introduce a public state, a status label, or a documentation claim that describes an unregistered subject as still owning a resource.
- Consumers MUST state the operation's declared bound and the relation between it and any client-side bound wherever either is documented, and a client bound MUST exceed the boundary bound it waits on.

## Rationale

Release-before-removal is the only ordering under which the prohibited state — an absent registration with a surviving owned resource — is unreachable rather than merely improbable. Removal-first can only be repaired by durable runtime state or by an out-of-band sweep, both of which are explicitly outside Ascend's accepted architecture. Doing the two steps in the right order but without exclusivity is equally unsound, because the window between them is exactly where a fresh acquisition installs a new generation for a still-registered subject.

Retirement is required because ordering alone does not stop a continuation that was already in flight when the claim was taken. A retirement set is the smallest mechanism that turns every such continuation into an explicit stale outcome, and it is sound precisely because durable identities are never reissued.

Injecting the effects the claiming boundary does not own, instead of giving it references to those boundaries, keeps one component responsible for ordering without creating a construction cycle and without letting a caller strand a claim it forgot to settle.

## Usage Examples

```
// Claiming boundary: one operation owns admission, order, bounds, and settlement.
const outcome = await lifecycle.close({
  projectId,
  drainConnections: (signal) => connections.closeProject(projectId, signal),
  commitRemoval: () => durable.closeProject(projectId),
})

// Phase order inside the claim, all deadlines armed from the claiming boundary's
// trusted synchronous scheduler:
//   1. drain connections for this subject only
//   2. resolve pending admissions and quarantined identities
//   3. release the exactly attributable owned resource
//   4. sweep every remaining ownership record for this subject
//   5. re-observe: auditConnections() synchronously; at most one extra drain inside
//      the same declared allowance; then one uninterrupted synchronous region
//      requiring zero residual across connections, ownership, admissions,
//      quarantine, in-flight operations, generation identity, and audit triples
//   6. commitRemoval()   <- next statement after step 5, no await between them
//   7. retire the identity and record the tombstone

// Every other path, while the claim is installed:
//   acquire()  -> bounded "closing" failure
//   release()  -> bounded "close-in-progress" rejection
//   replace()  -> bounded "close-in-progress" rejection
//   register() -> refused
```

## Integration Guidelines

- Place the claim, the phase order, the bounds, the confirmation predicate, and the retirement set inside the boundary that owns the volatile entries. Do not distribute them across a service, a route, or a client.
- Derive the operation's bound from the boundary's existing declared segments plus explicitly named new allowances, and assert the sum in code rather than in prose.
- Give every other lifecycle path exactly one new bounded, named rejection for a claimed subject, and publish it through the same route and client vocabularies those paths already use, so no claimed-subject refusal degrades into an uncategorised fault.
- Make the connection boundary's per-subject release take the caller's signal and return a per-subject audit; do not let it inherit or invent a timeout. If its resources publish no aggregate settlement event, pace its re-observation with one fixed gap that cancels its handle and removes its listener on settlement, and do not add a notification surface to resource maps that already answer a synchronous count.
- Prove exclusivity, ordering, joining, retirement, and interruption safety with executed scenarios against declared bounds, with negative controls that make each refusal class independently reachable, and with source guards that fail mechanically when a removal call becomes reachable without a preceding confirmation.

## Exceptions

- A subject that has never owned a volatile resource still follows the whole rule set; it simply satisfies confirmation with no signal. There is no fast path that skips the claim.
- A boundary shutdown may terminate and audit retained ownership outside a claim, because shutdown owns every subject at once and no removal is committed during it.
- A recovery boundary running before the operating boundary serves requests may install volatile state for durable subjects without a claim, because no removal is in flight; once it settles, the ordinary rules apply.

## Enforcement

- [x] Automated checks
  - A source guard asserts that the durable-removal callable is invoked from exactly one place in the claiming boundary, that the invocation is lexically dominated by the confirmation predicate, and that no route, service, client, or CLI calls durable removal directly.
  - A source guard asserts that every entry-installing site in the claiming boundary consults the retirement set and the claim map before installing, and fails a source in which a new install site is added without them.
  - A source guard asserts the single write authority against the mutation primitive itself: the volatile-state map mutation occurs exactly once in the boundary, inside the install helper, every other transition calls the helper, and no caller proceeds past a refused install. A guard expressed as a count of raw mutation-primitive occurrences inside one operation's body is prohibited, because it rejects the stronger single-authority implementation it exists to require.
  - A source guard asserts that the confirmation region sets the claim seal, that the write authority and the ownership registration both consult it, and that the seal is lifted only on the failed-removal branch immediately before the released-state install.
  - A source guard asserts that the claim's late-work counter is incremented only by a refused install, an ownership registration under a claim or after retirement, and an accounted stale settlement, and never by a refused acquisition, and that the boundary's refused-acquisition counter is reported by its audit and never read by the confirmation predicate.
  - A source guard asserts that the connection boundary's per-subject release owns no authority over time and leaves no handle behind: no interval, no scheduled deadline, no cancellation-signal timeout, no clock read, no elapsed arithmetic, and no attempt, retry, or accumulator counter; every scheduling call is the one fixed observation gap captured in a single handle; that handle is cancelled and the cancellation listener removed on the settlement path; every settlement is guarded by the subject's own zero-count predicate or the caller's abort; and every resource map is filtered by subject token. A guard expressed as a ban on **every** timer in that release is prohibited: it is unsatisfiable for a boundary whose resources publish no aggregate settlement event, and it would force either an invented notification surface or an unbounded synchronous spin.
  - A source guard asserts that no `await` appears between the final synchronous confirmation region and the durable-removal call, and that the sweep multiplication takes the clamped capped cardinality rather than a raw observed count.
  - A source guard asserts that every acquisition and join seam re-evaluates the claim after every `await` and before it installs an entry or returns a resource handle.
  - An executed scenario proves the pre-removal re-observation with two arrivals in declared disjoint roles, recording each arrival's own settlement, and the evidence validator rejects a row that records one arrival's settlement as another's or that carries more settlements of a kind than the production path produced. An executed interruption scenario records its recovery branch, the condition that decided it, and one signal account per episode phase, and the validator rejects an aggregate signal claim over the episode.
  - A source guard asserts that the participating connection boundary registers its per-subject operation before it requests an acquisition, and an executed scenario proves that an arrival refused by the claim after a completed release is still counted by the per-subject audit until it unwinds, so the confirmation re-observation is exercised on the ordinary path. A scenario that claims a live upstream connection created under an installed claim is rejected as unreachable unless it also removes a post-suspension claim re-evaluation, which is a negative control rather than a passing row.
  - A contract test asserts that the declared overall bound equals the sum of its named segments, that the release segment equals the clamped cardinality times the declared single-release bound, and that every segment and every product is a positive safe integer.
- [x] Code review checklist
  - Any new operation that deletes durable state declares its claim, its confirmation predicate, its bound, and its retirement behaviour in its own plan before implementation.
- [x] Test coverage requirements
  - Executed scenarios cover: confirmation before removal; a subject with no owned resources; unconfirmed release; removal failure after confirmed release; concurrent requests joining one claim; each contending lifecycle path refused; stale and reordered settlements after retirement; interruption before confirmation while a surviving attributable resource remains, interruption between confirmation and removal, and interruption after removal, each replayed on a real replacement boot that must prove no false deletion, exact attribution, no unauthorized signal, an ordered teardown, and a zero residual; a subject holding more than one retained ownership record with no quarantined identity; a frozen cardinality above the declared cap; peer isolation with an unrelated control resource; and a residual audit taken as an independent re-observation after capture.

## Revision — Plan revision 2, 2026-08-16

Three rules above were added after independent review of the first BL-020 plan revision found them missing, and one interface obligation was strengthened. Confirmation is now explicitly a synchronous re-observation immediately preceding removal, because the first revision confirmed against an audit taken several phases earlier that a concurrent acquisition could invalidate. Claim re-evaluation after every `await` was added, because refusing once at the head of an acquisition does not stop an acquisition that is already suspended. The release segment is now cardinality-aware with a declared cap and a fail-closed over-cap refusal, because a sweep over every ownership record is not bounded by a single-release allowance. The ordering, exclusivity, joining, retirement, interruption, filesystem, persistence, and evidence rules are otherwise unchanged.

## Revision — Plan revision 4, 2026-08-16 (during implementation)

Two rules were added after implementation of the first consumer of this component reached a contradiction between the confirmation predicate and the refusal mechanism the same component mandates. The first separates a **refused acquisition** from **late work**, because counting a refusal that produced nothing as outstanding work makes a correct operation unconfirmable whenever its exclusivity is exercised, and the second gives the boundary one write authority over its volatile-state map with an explicit refusal contract and a seal spanning confirmation and removal, because "every site consults the claim" is satisfiable by a boundary that still writes that map from many places and says nothing about arrivals after the predicate has been read. Nothing was relaxed: the confirmation predicate keeps every clause, the ordering, exclusivity, joining, cardinality, retirement, interruption, filesystem, persistence, and evidence rules are unchanged, and the sealed window is a new prohibition rather than a new permission.

## Revision — Plan revision 5, 2026-08-16 (during implementation)

One rule was added and one enforcement obligation was rewritten after the first consumer's evidence contract turned this component's own wording into a guard the delivered architecture cannot satisfy. The release-ordering rules said a participating boundary "MUST NOT self-time", and the enforcement section asked for a guard proving the per-subject release "contains no timer". Read literally, those forbid the release from ever re-reading its own counts — which is the only way it can observe settlement, because the connection boundary's resource maps are mutated from independent host and library callbacks and publish no aggregate settlement event. The prohibition is therefore stated as what it always meant: no **authority over time**. A fixed observation gap that decides only when to look again, that can neither end the wait nor extend it past the caller's signal, and whose handle and listener are released on settlement, is permitted and now carries explicit hygiene obligations. Nothing is relaxed: the caller's cancellation signal remains the release's only bound, the claiming boundary keeps the single timing origin, the confirmation predicate, ordering, exclusivity, joining, cardinality, retirement, interruption, filesystem, persistence, and evidence rules are unchanged, and the boundary now owes a handle-hygiene obligation it did not owe before.

## Revision — Plan revision 6, 2026-08-16 (during implementation)

One rule was added and one enforcement obligation was extended after the first consumer executed its scenario matrix against a real boundary and found that the choreography its plan declared for the re-observation proof was unreachable. The plan had justified the re-observation with an acquisition that passes the claim and then opens a new upstream connection after the release; the connection boundary registers its per-subject operation *before* it requests that acquisition, and the release does not settle until that count reaches zero, so no such connection can exist under a claim that refuses the acquisition. The component now states the ordering, states the residue a correctly refused arrival still leaves, and requires the proof to be built from that residue. Nothing is relaxed: the re-observation requirement is strengthened from "a defective acquisition might defeat the earlier audit" to "an ordinary refused arrival does", and the escaped-connection case is retained as the negative control it always was. The ordering, exclusivity, joining, cardinality, bounds, retirement, interruption, filesystem, persistence, and evidence rules, and the revision-2, revision-4, and revision-5 amendments, are unchanged.

## Revision — Plan revision 7, 2026-08-16 (during implementation)

Executing revision 6's own replacement choreography, and the interruption scenario beside it, showed that two rules were still under-specified in ways a proof could satisfy untruthfully. Both are amended above, in place. No ordering, exclusivity, joining, cardinality, bound, retirement, filesystem, persistence, or evidence rule is reversed.

1. **The re-observation proof needs two arrivals, not one.** The registration-before-acquisition ordering that makes the re-observation necessary also makes it impossible for one arrival to be both the refused post-suspension acquisition and the resource the re-observation still sees. The rule now requires the split, names the two roles, and prohibits recoupling them.

2. **An interruption's recovered state belongs to the recovery boundary.** The interruption rule already said that a survivor which *cannot* be excluded or attributed leaves the registration unresolved or failed. It did not say what happens when the survivor *can* be attributed, and a consumer read the silence as a prohibition on recovery — which would have contradicted the recovery contract it depends on. The rule now states that the recovery boundary settles such a survivor through its own contract, fixes the four invariants an interruption proof may assert unconditionally, and requires per-phase attribution of any count several phases can produce.
