**Revision 3** is a second mandatory return to Plan. A narrowed independent recheck of revision 2 confirmed one CRITICAL and two related defects, all repaired here before any code exists and all marked **[R3]**:

1. **CRITICAL — late ownership could materialise after the deadline and after a retry installed a successor.** `launchReadyRuntime` calls `onOwned` only after the awaited `process.launch()` resolves (`apps/api/src/project-runtime-process.ts:796-809`), so a deadline firing inside that window left a real process possible with no ownership record. Revision 2 then settled `Failed` / `restart-deadline-exceeded`, declared the project immediately restart-eligible, and classified a failed entry with no ownership record as `no-record` / already absent / gate passed. A second Restart could therefore install a healthy successor while the abandoned first launch later called `onOwned`, producing an untracked second live generation. The repair is a manager-owned **pending replacement admission** created synchronously before every `launch` call, a fourth `pending-admission` prior-resource class that the `no-record` shortcut may never absorb, and a gate rule that resolves the admission before any successor may be launched.
2. **HIGH — unguarded reuse of `start`'s `onOwned` / `onCleanup` wiring.** The delivered callbacks unconditionally `registerOwnership(projectId, generation, record)` and `recordCleanup(projectId, audit)`, and `recordCleanup` is project-keyed, so a late cleanup could overwrite a successor restart's evidence. The repair is restart-scoped callback wrappers with exact generation/entry/settlement guards, dedicated quarantine ownership and quarantine cleanup indexes keyed by admission identifier **plus** exact identity tuple, and the amendment of revision 2's absolute post-deadline prohibition into the honest constrained rule.
3. **MEDIUM/HIGH — shutdown could await an abandoned never-settling launch.** Revision 2 pointed Implement at `start`'s aborted-launch path, and `start` adds its operation to `completionTasks`, which manager shutdown drains in an unbounded `while` loop. The repair is a detached `void launchPromise.then(...)` late-settlement continuation that no awaited task set tracks, plus honest bounded shutdown semantics that report an unresolved admission instead of blocking on it and never claim a zero residual or a completed audit without a handle and an audit.

**Revision 4** is a third mandatory return to Plan. Final narrowed validation of revision 3 confirmed every earlier repair held but rejected it for exactly two collision-cleanup defects. Both are repaired here before any code exists and both are marked **[R4]**:

1. **Confirming collision cleanups left stale ownership records, contradicting the one-record guarantee.** `launchReadyRuntime` calls `onOwned` once per launch attempt and, on a port collision, terminates the attempt, calls `onCleanup`, and `continue`s to the next attempt (`apps/api/src/project-runtime-process.ts:789-893`). Revision 3's `restartOnOwned` registered every attempt's exact ownership record while `restartOnCleanup` only wrote the project-keyed `recordCleanup`, so no rule ever deleted a collision attempt's record. A three-attempt eventual success therefore left three ownership records where AC-11, V-3, and V-5 guarantee one. Revision 3 then papered over the symptom by declaring a failed entry's prior owned resources **plural**, which contradicted the singular prior handle and the exactly-one release termination stated everywhere else in the same document. The repair makes the installed `restartOnCleanup` branch identity-keyed: it derives the exact ownership key from the cleanup audit as `pid:processStartTime:port`, which is byte-identical to `ownershipKey(record)` and is exactly the derivation the delivered shutdown sweep already performs at `apps/api/src/project-runtime-manager.ts:799-805`, and deletes that one matching record under a generation guard when the audit reports all three absences. The singular prior handle, the exact-one release termination, and the exactly-one-ownership-record-on-success invariant are restored, and every plural prior-record clause introduced solely to accommodate retained collision attempts is removed.
2. **A non-confirming collision cleanup was discarded, so a later restart could claim residual-free over an unaudited residual.** The same wrapper wrote the audit project-keyed, where the next attempt's audit overwrote it, and `launchReadyRuntime` continued regardless. A collided attempt whose process, owned process group, or listener was never confirmed absent therefore left no durable evidence and no gate obstacle, and a later restart could settle `restarted` with `residualCount: 0` over it. The repair uses the same identity-keyed wrapper: a confirming triple removes the exact record; a non-confirming triple moves that exact identity into the existing admission-plus-identity quarantine model with `auditState: 'audited-unconfirmed'`, records its exact audit under the same quarantine key, and **blocks the current replacement attempt immediately** by aborting the restart's phase controller with exactly `new RuntimeFailure('restart-replacement-unconfirmed')`. `launchReadyRuntime`'s next loop-head `if (input.signal.aborted) throw abortFailure(input.signal)` observes that abort before `ports.acquire()` and before `process.launch(`, so one-runtime and one-listener hold by construction and no shared launch-primitive change is required. The operation settles `rejected` / `replacement-failed` with `replacementAuditState: 'quarantined-unconfirmed'` and makes no success and no zero-residual claim; reclamation is the existing bounded quarantine path — the next accepted restart's release phase inside `restartQuarantineReleaseBoundMs` (15,000 ms), or the manager shutdown sweep — and no restart may pass its gate while that project holds a quarantine record that is not `audited-absent`.

**[R4] Why blocking rather than continuing.** The two permitted shapes are to block the attempt loop immediately or to continue under a quarantined non-forwardable identity. Continuing is rejected: `auditRuntimeResource` derives `processAbsent` from the exact process start time, `processGroupAbsent` from owned-group membership, and `listenerAbsent` from a port probe (`apps/api/src/project-runtime-process.ts:255-292`), so a non-confirming triple can mean the collided attempt's own process or an owned descendant is still alive. Launching a successor over that would create a second live generation for the same project, canonical directory, and stable route, which is the precise failure AC-1, AC-9, and AC-11 forbid. Blocking costs the caller nothing it was promised: the attempt loop ends earlier than its allowance, no new wait is added to the current operation, and the user's next explicit Restart reclaims the quarantined identity inside the already-declared 15,000 ms quarantine allowance. Restart therefore refuses to build a new generation on top of an unaudited residual, exactly as it refuses to launch over an unconfirmed release.

## Restart Contract

### Manager operation

`ProjectRuntimeManager.restart({ projectId })` returns `Promise<RuntimeRestartOutcome>`. It is the only producer of an explicit replacement. It never throws for a classified lifecycle outcome; it rejects only on an infrastructure fault or `RuntimeRestartInvariantError`, which the route surfaces explicitly.

### Eligibility by installed entry

| Installed entry | Restart result | Public state before | Public state after |
|---|---|---|---|
| absent | `rejected` / `no-managed-runtime` | `Stopped` | `Stopped` |
| `registered`, not released | `rejected` / `no-managed-runtime` | `Stopped` | `Stopped` |
| `registered`, released by a confirmed stop | `rejected` / `no-managed-runtime` | `Stopped` | `Stopped` |
| `starting` | `rejected` / `start-in-progress` | `Starting` | `Starting` |
| `running` | accepted | `Running` | `Running` on success, `Failed` on either non-success |
| `stopping` | `rejected` / `stop-in-progress` | `Running` | `Running` |
| `restarting` | joins the in-flight operation | `Starting` | same as the joined operation |
| `failed` (current process) | accepted | `Failed` | `Running` on success, `Failed` on either non-success |
| any, while `shuttingDown` | `rejected` / `manager-shutdown` | unchanged | unchanged |
| project not persisted | `rejected` / `not-registered` | n/a | n/a |

A project reported `Stopped` is never restarted; it uses the delivered Open flow. This is the only reading consistent with AC-12, which lists a registered project reported `Stopped` among the bounded non-success cases.

### Pending replacement admission and quarantine **[R3]**

This section is the whole architecture of revision 3's critical repair. It is decided here, not left to Implement.

**Why it exists.** `launchReadyRuntime` acquires a port, awaits `dependencies.process.launch({...})`, and only then calls `input.onOwned?.({ process: owned, port })` (`apps/api/src/project-runtime-process.ts:796-809`). Between those two statements a real host process can exist while no manager record describes it. Revision 2 armed the overall deadline correctly but then classified the resulting retained failure from the ownership index alone, so an abandoned launch looked identical to a pre-spawn `spawn-error`: `priorResourceClass: 'no-record'`, release mode `already-absent`, gate passed. Because revision 2 also (correctly) keeps that retained failure immediately restart-eligible, a second Restart could pass its gate on an absence nobody observed and install a healthy successor while the first launch was still able to materialise a second live generation for the same project, the same canonical directory, and the same stable route. Ownership *absence* and ownership *ignorance* must stop being the same value.

**The record.** `PendingReplacementAdmission` is manager-owned state, not an entry, not a snapshot, and not a public state.

| Field | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `projectId` | `string` | stable project ID the admission belongs to |
| `restartGeneration` | `symbol` | the restart generation that created it |
| `admissionId` | `string` | opaque deterministic token `deriveProjectOwnerToken(projectId) + ':a' + <per-manager monotonic counter>`; carries no path, PID, or port |
| `canonicalPath` | `string` | the path handed to `launch` |
| `ownerToken` | `string` | the owner token handed to `launch` |
| `admittedAt` | `number` | monotonic `deadlineScheduler.now()` reading taken in the same synchronous step as creation |
| `phase` | `RestartAdmissionPhase` | one of exactly four values, below |
| `ownedReported` | `boolean` | whether any ownership callback has fired for this admission |
| `resolution` | `Promise<void>` | resolves when `phase` leaves `launch-pending`; created with its resolver captured, never awaited by the creating restart |
| `resolvedPhase` | `RestartAdmissionPhase \| undefined` | the terminal phase, set once |

`RESTART_ADMISSION_PHASES` is frozen with exactly **4** members in this order: `launch-pending`, `materialized-quarantined`, `absent-confirmed`, `audited-absent`.

**Storage and cardinality.** `pendingAdmissions: Map<string, PendingReplacementAdmission>` keyed by stable project ID. At most **one** admission exists per project at any moment, because a restart may create one only after every earlier admission for that project has resolved *and* been consumed. Creation is synchronous and immediately precedes the `launch` call; there is no path that calls `launch` first. A `FailedEntry` gains one optional field `pendingAdmissionId?: string`, so a retained failure references its unresolved admission by opaque token while the entry itself stays frozen and the mutable phase lives in the map. `ProjectRuntimeEntryInspection` gains `pendingAdmissionId?: string` and `pendingAdmissionPhase?: RestartAdmissionPhase`.

**Quarantine.** `quarantinedOwnership: Map<string, QuarantinedOwnership>` and `quarantineCleanups: Map<string, RuntimeTerminationAudit>`, both keyed by `restartQuarantineKey(admissionId, record) = admissionId + '|' + ownershipKey(record)` — exact identity **and** restart generation, never project ID alone. `QuarantinedOwnership extends RuntimeOwnershipRecord` with `projectId`, `restartGeneration`, `admissionId`, and `auditState`. `RESTART_QUARANTINE_AUDIT_STATES` is frozen with exactly **4** members: `unaudited`, `reclaiming`, `audited-absent`, `audited-unconfirmed`.

**Restart-scoped callback wrappers.** The restart passes `restartOnOwned(admission)` and `restartOnCleanup(admission)`, never `start`'s callbacks. Each reads one synchronous guard, `installed = !settled && entries.get(projectId) === restartingEntry`, where `settled` is a plain `let` the settlement sets to `true` before any await.

| Callback | `installed === true` | `installed === false` (late) |
|---|---|---|
| `onOwned(record)` | `registerOwnership(projectId, restartGeneration, record)`; set `admission.ownedReported = true` | `quarantineOwnership(admission, record)` with `auditState: 'unaudited'`; set `admission.ownedReported = true` |
| `onCleanup(audit)` | **[R4]** identity-keyed, per the rule below: derive `key = auditIdentityKey(audit)`, store the exact audit in `replacementAttemptAudits[key]`, then either delete exactly that ownership record (confirming triple) or move it into quarantine as `audited-unconfirmed` and abort the phase (non-confirming triple). **[R4] Identity-keyed replacement cleanup — the whole of revision 4's repair.** `launchReadyRuntime` calls `onOwned` once per launch attempt and, on a port collision, terminates that attempt, calls `onCleanup`, and continues to the next attempt; on any other failure it terminates, calls `onCleanup`, and rethrows (`apps/api/src/project-runtime-process.ts:789-893`). Every one of those callbacks therefore describes **one exact identity**, and the restart treats it that way.

```
auditIdentityKey(audit) = [audit.pid, audit.processStartTime, audit.port].join(':')
```

That expression is byte-identical to `ownershipKey(record)` and is exactly the derivation the delivered manager shutdown sweep already performs when it decides whether a prior audit describes the record it is holding (`apps/api/src/project-runtime-manager.ts:799-805`). No new key shape is invented.

|---|---|---|---|
| `processAbsent && processGroupAbsent && listenerAbsent` | `ownership.delete(key)` when `ownership.get(key)?.generation === restartGeneration`, exactly as the delivered confirmed-stop path deletes at `project-runtime-manager.ts:484-487`; at most one record is deleted and never another generation's | none | continues; the next attempt may acquire a port and launch normally |
| any other triple | the same guarded `ownership.delete(key)` — the identity **moves**, it is not duplicated | `quarantineOwnership(admission, identity)` with `auditState: 'audited-unconfirmed'` plus `recordQuarantineCleanup(admission, audit)` under `restartQuarantineKey(admissionId, key)` | **blocked**: the wrapper synchronously calls `phaseController.abort(new RuntimeFailure('restart-replacement-unconfirmed'))` |

**[R5] Why the abort blocks deterministically, and why the manager — not the launch primitive — decides the reason.** Revision 4 stated that "in both branches the abort ends the launch with the typed `RuntimeFailure` the wrapper supplied." **That statement was false and is withdrawn.** Reading the delivered source exactly:

| Branch in `launchReadyRuntime` | What ends the launch | What the launch promise rejects with |
|---|---|---|
| collision on an attempt before the last (`project-runtime-process.ts:841` `onCleanup`, `break`, `:870` `continue`) | the next iteration's loop head `if (input.signal.aborted) throw abortFailure(input.signal)` at `:794`, before `dependencies.ports.acquire()` at `:795` and before `dependencies.process.launch(` at `:799` | exactly the wrapper's `RuntimeFailure('restart-replacement-unconfirmed')`, because `abortFailure` returns `signal.reason` when it is a `RuntimeFailure` |
| collision on the **last** configured attempt | the `for` loop's own exhaustion — `continue` leaves no further iteration, so no further loop-head signal check runs | `failure('address-in-use-exhausted')` at `:890` — **not** the wrapper's reason |
| readiness, health, or early-exit failure (`:876` `catch`, `:882` `onCleanup`, `:883` rethrow) | the rethrow itself, which exits the function before any further iteration | `error instanceof RuntimeFailure ? error : …` at `:883-888`, so an already-typed `readiness-timeout`, `health-status-unexpected`, `health-body-unexpected`, `early-exit-code`, or `early-exit-signal` is rethrown **verbatim** and the wrapper's abort is never consulted |
| a non-`RuntimeFailure` error raised inside the readiness block | the same rethrow | `abortFailure(input.signal)` at `:886`, which **is** the wrapper's reason, because `input.signal.aborted` is already true |

**Blocking holds on every branch** — no further port is acquired, no further process is spawned, and no listener is created, either because the loop head throws before `ports.acquire()` or because the rethrow exits the function — but the **reason** the launch rejects with is the wrapper's typed reason on only two of the four branches. `launchReadyRuntime` stays unchanged, exactly as the Scope Boundaries section requires; the other half of the repair belongs to the manager and is fixed here.

**[R5] Restart settlement reason selector.** The restart's own `phaseController` is the single authority for why an accepted restart ended, and the settlement reads it **before** it reads the caught launch error. Two restart-owned values make that exact and total:

- `let replacementBlockReason: RuntimeFailure | undefined` — set exactly once, synchronously, by `restartOnCleanup`'s non-confirming branch to the *same instance* it passes to `phaseController.abort(...)`, assigned in the same synchronous step and before the abort call.
- the delivered `phaseSignal` and its `reason`.

At settlement of the replacement phase the two classification axes are selected as follows. Neither is a broad catch, a string match, an `instanceof Error` widening, or a success-shaped fallback; both are total over their inputs.

| Axis | Selector |
|---|---|
| **Retained failure category** | If `phaseSignal.aborted && phaseSignal.reason instanceof RuntimeFailure`, the retained category is exactly `phaseSignal.reason.category`, and the caught launch error is **never** consulted for classification. Otherwise it is the caught launch error's own category, exactly as the delivered path classifies it; a caught value that is neither a `RuntimeFailure` nor an aborted typed reason is an infrastructure fault and propagates, as it already does. |
| **`replacementAuditState`** | In this fixed order: `quarantined-unconfirmed` when `replacementBlockReason !== undefined`; else `admission-unresolved` when the launch never settled and `admission.ownedReported === false`; else `unaudited-retained` when ownership was reported and no completed confirming audit exists; else `audited-absent` when every attempt's audit confirmed all three absences; else `none` when no replacement phase ran. |

Because `AbortController.abort` keeps the **first** reason and ignores later ones, `phaseSignal.reason` is itself the deterministic tiebreak between the wrapper and the overall deadline: whichever aborted first is the reason, and the settlement never infers an ordering from timing. Two consequences are stated rather than left implicit:

- A readiness, health, early-exit, or last-attempt-collision failure whose cleanup did not confirm absence settles with retained category `restart-replacement-unconfirmed` and `replacementAuditState: 'quarantined-unconfirmed'`, exactly as a mid-loop collision does. This is the outcome T-2's settlement always intended; revision 4 asserted it as a property of `launchReadyRuntime`, which it is not, and revision 5 supplies the mechanism that actually produces it.
- If the overall deadline aborted **before** the wrapper, the retained category is `restart-deadline-exceeded` — honestly what ended the operation — while `replacementAuditState` is still `quarantined-unconfirmed`, because a quarantine record this operation created is a residual it holds whatever ended it. The row records both, and its `residualCount` is `null` either way.

The caught launch error is never discarded. The settlement records it on the evidence row as `replacementAttempts.launchRejectionCategory`, alongside `replacementAttempts.settlementReasonSource`, so an inspector sees both what the launch reported and what the manager classified, and the validator can prove the precedence held rather than taking it on trust. The T-8 source guard `restart-settlement-reason-precedence-missing` rejects any replacement-phase settlement branch that derives a retained category from the caught launch error without first reading `phaseSignal.reason` as a typed `RuntimeFailure`.

**What the blocked restart may and may not claim.** It settles `rejected` / `replacement-failed` — `RUNTIME_RESTART_REJECTION_CATEGORIES` stays at **7** — with the retained failure category `restart-replacement-unconfirmed`, `replacementAuditState: 'quarantined-unconfirmed'`, and the exact quarantine record listed in its evidence. It may claim: the caller settled inside its declared bound; no successor was installed; every attempt's exact identity is either confirmed absent or quarantined by exact identity with its audit retained; and no later restart can pass its gate until that record is reclaimed. It may **not** claim zero residual, success, `restarted`, or an absence for the quarantined identity. M-8 rejects any row, artifact, notice, or document that does.

**Reclamation is the existing bounded quarantine path, not a new mechanism.** The current operation performs no reclamation; the next accepted restart's release phase claims the record with the same synchronous compare-and-set from `audited-unconfirmed` to `reclaiming`, terminates and audits it exactly once, and deletes it only when that audit reports all three absences — inside `restartQuarantineReleaseBoundMs` (**15,000 ms**), which is already inside the **20,000 ms** release variant and the **81,000 ms** ceiling. The manager shutdown sweep is the other authority. No timer, interval, or background loop is added, and no bound value changes.

**Cardinality of the identity-keyed path.** At most `collisionAttempts` cleanup audits per replacement phase, each for a distinct identity, so `replacementAttemptAudits` can never overwrite an entry. At most **one** of those audits is non-confirming, because the first one aborts the loop. At most one quarantine record is therefore created per installed replacement phase, and at most `collisionAttempts` per project at any instant once an abandoned launch's late callbacks are included — which is exactly the population `restartQuarantineReleaseBoundMs` was already derived to cover.

**`replacementAttemptAudits` is restart state, not a manager index.** It is a `Map<string, RuntimeTerminationAudit>` owned by the restart operation, read by the settlement into the trusted result, and discarded with the operation. The manager's index count stays at three and `audit()` stays at sixteen members. The project-keyed `recordCleanup(projectId, audit)` is written by the restart body exactly **once per phase** — the prior-generation audit in the release phase, and the decisive replacement audit at settlement — never by a wrapper and never once per attempt, so `lastCleanup(projectId)` keeps its delivered "last cleanup for this project" meaning and a successor's evidence is never overwritten by a predecessor's collision cleanup.

**Permitted and prohibited late mutations.** A late callback and the detached continuation may write **only**: `quarantinedOwnership`, `quarantineCleanups`, `pendingAdmissions[projectId].phase` / `resolvedPhase` / `ownedReported`, the admission's resolver, and the `lateReplacementSettlements` counter. They MUST NOT write `entries`, `ownership`, `cleanupOutcomes` (that is, `lastCleanup(projectId)`), any successor snapshot or route, any lifecycle event, `completionTasks`, `backgroundTasks`, `restartTasks`, or `stopTasks`.

**[R3] Amended rule.** Revision 2 said "nothing after the deadline may register/remove ownership or record cleanup". That was too strong to be implementable safely: a blanket no-op leaks a real process. The honest constrained rule, which the ADR, the core-component, the guards, and the validator all now state identically, is: **after settlement no successor, entry, current-operation ownership record, current-operation cleanup record, or lifecycle event may be mutated, and exact late-resource quarantine and quarantine cleanup are permitted and required.**

**Detached late-settlement continuation.** When a restart settles with its launch still in flight, it attaches exactly one detached observer and adds the launch promise to **no** awaited set:

```
void launchPromise.then(
  (ready) => { lateReplacementSettlements += 1; onLateLaunchResolved(admission, ready) },
  (error) => { lateReplacementSettlements += 1; onLateLaunchRejected(admission, error) },

```

**Prior-resource classification (5 values).** **[R4]** `priorResourceClass` becomes `live-record`, `absent-record`, `no-record`, `pending-admission`, `quarantined-residual`. `no-record` is permitted **only** when `pendingAdmissions.has(projectId)` is false **and** no `quarantinedOwnership` value carries that `projectId` with an `auditState` other than `audited-absent`. An entry whose project holds an unresolved admission is always `pending-admission`; an entry whose project holds no unresolved admission but at least one quarantine record that is not `audited-absent` is always `quarantined-residual`; no code path may collapse either to `no-record`. Both classes are ignorance about a real resource, and ignorance is never absence — the same principle revision 3 established for admissions, applied to the residual a blocked replacement leaves behind. Quarantine records carry `projectId`, so the classification and the gate enumerate them by scanning `quarantinedOwnership.values()` for that project; the index is bounded at `collisionAttempts` records per project, so the scan is bounded by construction and no entry field, inspection field, or manager index is added.

**Retry release/gate resolution.** A restart whose prior-resource class is `pending-admission` performs the ordinary release work for every ownership record under the prior generation *and* awaits `admission.resolution`, raced against the same release backstop and no other timer:

### Retained-failure ownership

**[R4]** The prior owned resources a restart releases are **exactly one live handle** — the `ready` handle of a `running` entry, or the single ownership record registered under a `failed` entry's generation — together with any unresolved pending admission that entry references and any quarantine record its project still holds. For every generation a **restart** created, that handle is singular by construction, not by hope: each launch attempt's installed cleanup callback either deletes its exact identity from `ownership` on a confirming triple or moves it into quarantine on a non-confirming one, and it does so synchronously **before** the attempt loop can reach the next `ports.acquire()`. Revision 3's plural definition — introduced only to accommodate retained collision attempts, and contradicting the singular prior handle, the exactly-one release termination, and AC-11's one-record guarantee stated elsewhere in this document — is withdrawn. One delivered exception is recorded honestly rather than absorbed: a generation created by the delivered `start()` path may carry an extra stale ownership record, because `start` passes `registerOwnership`/`recordCleanup` directly and its project-keyed cleanup deletes nothing, so a collided attempt's record survives. That record is not a live handle — `launchReadyRuntime` already terminated and audited that attempt before returning — the release phase does not terminate it, and it does not gate anything; it is a delivered condition named in *Proof ceilings* below, and BL-018 neither repairs nor inherits it. Five bounded cases:

1. **`live-record`** — for example a `stop-unconfirmed` retention. The release phase terminates and audits that exact identity like any other. Where a record already carries an exact cleanup audit confirming all three absences, the gate reuses it exactly as the delivered shutdown sweep reuses a confirming prior audit, rather than terminating a confirmed-absent identity again.
2. **`absent-record`** — the audit confirms all three absences with no signal delivered. Release mode `already-absent`; the gate passes.
3. **`no-record`** — for example a pre-launch `spawn-error` or `executable-missing` failure. The release phase performs one bounded confirmation audit of nothing to release, records release mode `already-absent` with `signalDelivery: not-attempted`, and the gate passes. **[R3] This class is now permitted only when `pendingAdmissions.has(projectId)` is false**, **[R4]** and only when that project holds no quarantine record outside `audited-absent`.
4. **`pending-admission`** **[R3]** — the entry references an admission that has not resolved, so the manager knows a launch was started and does not know what it created. This is never `no-record` and never `already-absent`. It resolves through the retry release/gate table above.
5. **`quarantined-residual`** **[R4]** — the project holds no unresolved admission, but at least one quarantine record for it is not `audited-absent`: a replacement attempt was terminated and its audit did not confirm all three absences. The manager holds an exact identity and an exact audit, so this is neither ignorance of a handle nor a confirmed absence. It resolves through the quarantined-residual release/gate table above, and until it does, no restart for that project may launch a replacement or report a zero residual.

A retained failure is never a reason to skip release and never a reason to fail a restart whose prior resources are provably gone. Eligibility persists across repeated unsuccessful restarts until one succeeds or the manager shuts down; revision 3 gates the replacement, never the acceptance.

### Synchronous claim and joining

Restart awaits `findProjectById` first, then runs one synchronous section that reads the installed entry and, for `running` or `failed`, installs the `restarting` entry. No `await` occurs between that read and that install. A caller that reads a `restarting` entry returns `entry.operation`. Eight concurrent callers therefore produce one claim, one release phase, one replacement launch, one `runtime.restart.requested`, one terminal event, and eight identical settled results.

`RestartingEntry` carries: `state: 'restarting'`, `projectId`, `canonicalPath`, `generation` (the new restart generation symbol), `priorGeneration` (the replaced generation symbol, or `undefined` when the failed entry had none), `prior` (the `ReadyRuntime` when the entry was `running`, the retained ownership record when the failed entry had one, otherwise `undefined`), `priorSnapshot` (the complete pre-restart snapshot), `snapshot` (a `starting`-state snapshot for the restart), `controller`, and `operation`.

### Phases and the gate

1. **Claim** — synchronous, described above. Emits `runtime.restart.requested` with `from` = `running` or `failed` and `to` = `restarting`.
2. **Release phase** **[R2][R3][R4]** — terminates the prior generation's single live handle through `terminate(gracefulShutdownMs, forceShutdownMs, port, phaseSignal)` — exactly one termination, exactly as the delivered selected stop terminates exactly the entry's own `ready` handle — and, when the prior-resource class is `pending-admission` or `quarantined-residual`, additionally awaits `admission.resolution` and performs any claimed quarantine reclamation. The whole phase is raced against the *release backstop*, an `AbortSignal`-derived promise fired by `deadlineScheduler.scheduleDeadline(releaseBoundMs, () => phaseController.abort(new RuntimeFailure('restart-release-unconfirmed')))`, where `releaseBoundMs` is chosen once, synchronously, at acceptance from the single boolean **[R4]** `requiresQuarantineResolution = pendingAdmissions.has(projectId) || projectHasUnclearedQuarantine(projectId)`: `runtimeStopOverallBoundMs(config)` when it is false and `runtimeRestartReleaseBoundMs(config)` when it is true. A quarantine record costs exactly the reclamation work an admission costs, so it arms the same already-declared allowance rather than a new one. `processDependencies.sleep` is **not** used, and the restart still holds exactly two `scheduleDeadline` arms. With no prior handle, no admission, and no quarantine record, performs one bounded confirmation and records `already-absent`.
3. **Gate** — passes only when the release audit reports `processAbsent && processGroupAbsent && listenerAbsent` for the complete pre-restart identity **[R3]** *and* for every quarantined identity the resolved admission produced, and only when no admission for that project remains unresolved. **[R4]** It additionally requires that **no** quarantine record for that project is in any state other than `audited-absent`, whatever created it — a late callback, a detached continuation, or an installed replacement attempt that was blocked. This is the rule that stops a later restart from claiming residual-free over a collision attempt whose process group or listener was never confirmed absent. Nothing is spawned, no port is acquired, and no launch is scheduled before it passes. **[R2]** Passing the gate sets a synchronous `gateConfirmed` boolean on the restart's own state before the replacement phase begins; that boolean is the only input to the deadline's phase-aware settlement branch and is recorded on every evidence row.
4. **Replacement phase** **[R3]** — first creates the `PendingReplacementAdmission` synchronously and inserts it into `pendingAdmissions`, then calls the same `launch` used by `start`, with the same `config` object — including its `collisionAttempts` — the same canonical path, owner token, and `dependencies`, under the restart's phase signal, and with `restartOnOwned(admission)` / `restartOnCleanup(admission)` in place of `start`'s callbacks. There is no control-flow path that reaches `launch(` without having inserted its admission first, and the T-8 source guard rejects one.
5. **Settlement** — rechecks that the exact `restarting` object is still installed, sets `settled = true` synchronously, then installs exactly one terminal entry and emits exactly one terminal event. **[R3]** When the launch is still in flight at this point, the settlement additionally attaches the one detached `void launchPromise.then(...)` continuation, records `pendingAdmissionId` on the retained `failed` entry, and adds the launch promise to no awaited task set. When the launch has already settled, the admission is resolved and deleted in this same synchronous step.

No entry is ever left in `restarting`. `Running` is never reported for an unconfirmed release or an unready replacement. **[R3]** No settlement reports an absence, a completed audit, or a zero residual for a replacement whose launch never reported ownership; that case is `admission-unresolved` and is honest about what was never observed. **[R4]** No settlement reports an absence or a zero residual for an identity whose exact audit did not confirm all three absences; that case is `quarantined-unconfirmed`, the identity is retained under its exact quarantine key with its audit, and only a later confirming audit — from the next restart's claimed reclamation or the shutdown sweep — may delete it. **[R5]** In every one of those cases the row's `residualCount` is `null` rather than `0`: `null` is the schema's only representation of a withheld residual claim, and no row, notice, event, artifact, or document may substitute a zero for it. **[R5]** The retained category and `replacementAuditState` of any replacement-phase settlement are produced by the *Restart settlement reason selector*, which reads the restart's own `phaseSignal.reason` before the caught launch error, so a readiness, health, early-exit, or last-attempt-collision failure whose cleanup did not confirm absence retains `restart-replacement-unconfirmed` exactly as a mid-loop collision does.

**[R2] Deadline settlement rules.** The overall deadline aborts `phaseController` with exactly `new RuntimeFailure('restart-deadline-exceeded')`. That typed reason matters: `launchReadyRuntime`'s `abortFailure(signal)` returns `signal.reason` when it is a `RuntimeFailure` and otherwise fabricates `manager-shutdown`, so an untyped abort would make a healthy manager report itself shut down through the launch failure, the terminal event classification, the public `failureCategory`, the Home notice, and the retained evidence row simultaneously. Which settlement the deadline produces is decided **only** by `gateConfirmed`, never inferred from timing. A deadline before the gate launches nothing and is indistinguishable in result, retained category, ownership, and evidence from any other unconfirmed release, which is what AC-8 requires. A deadline after the gate reuses the existing `replacement-failed` operation category — so `RUNTIME_RESTART_REJECTION_CATEGORIES` stays at **7** and the route mapping `replacement-failed -> runtime_replacement_failed -> 500` is unchanged — and is distinguished by the retained `restart-deadline-exceeded` category exactly as a spawn-class failure is distinguished by `spawn-error`. The retained entry is `Failed`, carries `restart-deadline-exceeded` in the public projection, is immediately eligible for another explicit Restart, and is swept by manager shutdown through the ordinary retained-ownership path. Nothing that settles after the deadline may install an entry, mutate an entry, register or remove an ownership record, record a cleanup for the settled restart, emit an event, or become routable: a late launch is terminated and audited and counted in `lateReplacementSettlements`; a late termination is counted in `lateTerminationSettlements`; a late health or exit settlement loses its guarded compare-and-set.

`restartSettlementAllowanceMs` is an internal runtime configuration field with a fixed default, set only through `createProjectRuntimeConfig` overrides in validation. It is not an environment variable, flag, secret, or deployment setting, and it is validated as a positive safe integer exactly like its siblings. Both derived bounds are pure functions of `ProjectRuntimeConfig`:

```
runtimeReplacementBoundMs(config)        = config.collisionAttempts * (config.readinessTimeoutMs + runtimeStopOverallBoundMs(config))
restartQuarantineReleaseBoundMs(config)  = config.collisionAttempts * runtimeStopOverallBoundMs(config)
runtimeRestartReleaseBoundMs(config)     = runtimeStopOverallBoundMs(config) + restartQuarantineReleaseBoundMs(config)
runtimeRestartOverallBoundMs(config, requiresQuarantineResolution) =
  (requiresQuarantineResolution ? runtimeRestartReleaseBoundMs(config) : runtimeStopOverallBoundMs(config))
  + runtimeReplacementBoundMs(config)
  + config.restartSettlementAllowanceMs
```

At the delivered defaults: `3 * (15,000 + 5,000) = 60,000`; `3 * 5,000 = 15,000`; `5,000 + 15,000 = 20,000`; `5,000 + 60,000 + 1,000 = 66,000` when nothing must be resolved and `20,000 + 60,000 + 1,000 = 81,000` when an admission or a quarantine record must be. **[R4] No bound value changes in revision 4.** The second parameter is renamed from `hasPendingAdmission` to `requiresQuarantineResolution` and its definition widens from `pendingAdmissions.has(projectId)` to `pendingAdmissions.has(projectId) || projectHasUnclearedQuarantine(projectId)`, because reclaiming a quarantine record costs exactly the work `restartQuarantineReleaseBoundMs` was already derived to cover — one self-bounded sequencer termination per record, at most `collisionAttempts` records per project. A blocked replacement adds **no** wait to its own operation: it aborts its attempt loop early, so it consumes strictly less of `runtimeReplacementBoundMs` than the attempts it skipped were allotted, and it performs no reclamation of its own.

**[R3] Why the quarantine allowance is exactly `collisionAttempts * runtimeStopOverallBoundMs`.** One abandoned launch can create at most one owned identity per configured attempt, because `launchReadyRuntime` acquires one port and calls `process.launch` at most once per iteration. **[R4]** An installed replacement phase adds at most **one** further quarantined identity, because the first non-confirming cleanup aborts the attempt loop, and its own record leaves `ownership` in the same synchronous step, so the population the allowance must cover is unchanged at `collisionAttempts` records per project. Each quarantined identity receives one termination from the detached continuation and at most one claimed re-attempt from the resolving restart, and each of those terminations is self-bounded by the sequencer at `runtimeStopOverallBoundMs`. The resolving restart therefore needs at most `collisionAttempts` sequencer bounds on top of its ordinary prior-generation release, and that is exactly the allowance. **[R4]** `requiresQuarantineResolution` is read once, synchronously, at acceptance, and is the only input other than `ProjectRuntimeConfig` that either bound function accepts; the T-8 source guard rejects any other input.

**[R2] Why the replacement allowance is not `readinessTimeoutMs`.** `launchReadyRuntime` iterates `attempt = 1..config.collisionAttempts`. Each attempt acquires a port, launches, and polls readiness for a full `readinessTimeoutMs` measured from that attempt's own `dependencies.now()` reading. A collision runs `owned.terminate(gracefulShutdownMs, forceShutdownMs, port)` and `continue`s; a failure runs the same terminate in its `catch` and rethrows. Both cleanup terminations are self-bounded by the sequencer at `runtimeStopOverallBoundMs` and are called **without** a caller signal, so the restart cannot cancel them. Only after all attempts are exhausted does the loop throw `address-in-use-exhausted`. The honest worst case is therefore `collisionAttempts x (readinessTimeoutMs + runtimeStopOverallBoundMs)`, not `readinessTimeoutMs`.

**[R2] Why restart keeps the delivered launch configuration.** Restart passes the same `config` object `start` passes, with `collisionAttempts` unchanged. Deriving a single-attempt configuration for restart would diverge restart from Open for no safety gain, turn a recoverable transient loopback-port race into a terminal restart failure, and make `address-in-use-exhausted` unreachable from a restart while it stays reachable from Open — an asymmetry that would then need its own vocabulary, evidence, and documentation. `address-in-use-exhausted` therefore stays honestly reachable from a restart and is exercised by scenario 50.

**[R2] Deadline arming: exact boundary, default, injection, cancellation, and clock.**

- **Boundary.** `RuntimeDeadlineScheduler` in `apps/api/src/project-runtime-process.ts`, with exactly two members: `now(): number` and `scheduleDeadline(milliseconds: number, onDeadline: () => void): () => void`. `RuntimeTerminationPrimitives extends RuntimeDeadlineScheduler`, so the sequencer's trusted pair and the manager's deadline pair are one contract that cannot drift.
- **Default implementation.** `defaultRuntimeDeadlineScheduler: RuntimeDeadlineScheduler = defaultRuntimeTerminationPrimitives` — the delivered trusted implementation itself, i.e. the monotonic `performance.now()` clock and a native `setTimeout` whose returned handle clears it.
- **Injection.** `ProjectRuntimeManagerDependencies` gains one optional `deadlineScheduler?: RuntimeDeadlineScheduler` defaulting to `defaultRuntimeDeadlineScheduler`. Only `restart` reads it. The manager is given no signal-delivery, `/proc`-reading, or listener-probing primitive.
- **Test shape.** Validation injects a recording scheduler that captures every `(milliseconds, onDeadline)` pair, returns a cancellation spy, and lets a test fire a specific deadline synchronously. This is the same technique the delivered sequencer tests already use through `RuntimeTerminationPrimitives`; no real timer is waited on, so neither the 66,000 ms nor the 81,000 ms bound costs any wall-clock time in the matrix.
- **Cancellation.** Each arm's returned handle is captured and invoked on every exit path — release settlement for the release backstop, restart settlement for the overall deadline, and the `finally` of the operation for both — so no timer, listener, or unhandled rejection survives a restart.
- **Clock.** Deadline arming passes a duration, so no clock arithmetic is required to arm. `deadlineScheduler.now()` supplies the monotonic elapsed values written to the disposable timing artifact and used for `withinDeclaredBound`. The manager's existing `now` dependency continues to supply `elapsedMs` on lifecycle events; that delivered behaviour is unchanged.
- **Prohibited.** `processDependencies.sleep`, `RuntimeTerminationPrimitives.delay`, and any other fallible awaited primitive MUST NOT bound any restart phase, gate, or settlement. A source guard enforces this over the restart function body. The readiness poll inside `launchReadyRuntime` keeps its delivered `dependencies.sleep(pollIntervalMs, signal)` call; that is inside the launch contract, not the restart's deadline mechanism, and the guard is scoped accordingly.
- **Delivered ceiling, recorded not inherited.** The delivered selected-stop backstop still races `processDependencies.sleep(runtimeStopOverallBoundMs(config), signal)`. BL-018 does not change it and does not repair it. No BL-018 document, evidence row, or notice may describe the delivered stop backstop as trusted-scheduler armed.

**[R2] Race shape.** Each deadline resolves an `AbortSignal`-derived promise, not a dependency promise: `scheduleDeadline` fires `onDeadline`, which synchronously aborts `phaseController` with a typed `RuntimeFailure`, which resolves the raced promise from the signal's `abort` listener. Nothing in that chain awaits fallible work, so a `terminate` or `launch` that never settles or ignores its signal cannot delay the restart's own settlement.

**[R2] Proof ceiling on the replacement.** The replacement allowance is arithmetic over configured allowances; `assertLaunchable`, `ports.acquire`, and `process.launch` are still awaited without bounds of their own. The guarantee is the caller-visible one: the restart settles within `runtimeRestartOverallBoundMs` regardless. When the launch primitive respects cancellation, its own failure path terminates and audits the owned replacement, the settlement records `replacementAuditState: 'audited-absent'` with a completed three-absence audit, and AC-9's zero-residual claim is proven. When the launch primitive ignores cancellation or never settles, the restart records `replacementAuditState: 'unaudited-retained'`, keeps the ownership record registered through `onOwned`, and defers exact cleanup to the single manager-shutdown ownership sweep. **[R5]** Every row with `replacementAuditState: 'unaudited-retained'` MUST record `residualCount: null` — the schema's sole representation of a withheld claim — and the validator rejects both a `0` there and a `null` on a row whose exact audit did observe absence.

### Cancellation and cleanup cardinality

- One phase `AbortController` is created per accepted restart and is aborted by the release backstop, by the overall deadline, by manager shutdown, and by the restart's own return. **[R2]** Every abort carries a typed `RuntimeFailure` reason: `restart-release-unconfirmed` from the release backstop, `restart-deadline-exceeded` from the overall deadline, and `manager-shutdown` from shutdown. **[R2]** Both deadline arms are `scheduleDeadline` handles cancelled on every exit path; the restart holds no other timer.
- **Restart-release phase:** exactly one termination and at most one cleanup audit for the prior generation. **[R4]** Exactly one, because at most one ownership record exists per generation.
- **Restart-replacement phase:** **[R4]** at most one termination and one identity-keyed cleanup audit **per launch attempt**, so at most `collisionAttempts` in total, each for a distinct exact identity and each stored under its own key in `replacementAttemptAudits` where nothing can overwrite it. At most one of them is non-confirming, because the first non-confirming audit aborts the attempt loop. Exactly one **project-keyed** `recordCleanup(projectId, ...)` is written per phase — the prior audit in the release phase and the decisive replacement audit at settlement — so `lastCleanup(projectId)` keeps its delivered meaning and no attempt overwrites another's evidence.
- A prior generation whose release ended unconfirmed, faulted, or deadline-abandoned retains its ownership record and is re-attempted exactly once by the manager shutdown sweep, per the delivered rule that shutdown reuses a prior audit only when that audit confirms all three absences. This is the intended second phase, never an uncontrolled duplicate.
- A confirmed release removes the prior ownership record, so no shutdown re-attempt occurs for it.
- **[R3] Admission cardinality.** At most one admission per stable project ID; exactly one admission created per accepted restart that reaches its replacement phase; exactly one deletion per admission. **[R4]** The deletion is performed by exactly one of three authorities and never twice: the owning restart's own settlement when its launch settled before settlement (success or failure), a later restart's gate phase, or the manager shutdown sweep. It is never performed by the detached continuation. A joined caller creates none.
- **[R3] Quarantine cardinality.** Exactly one termination attempt per quarantined exact identity from the path that quarantined it — **[R4]** the detached continuation for a late identity, and `launchReadyRuntime`'s own cleanup termination for an installed replacement attempt, which is the audit the wrapper reads — plus at most one re-attempt claimed by a synchronous compare-and-set from `audited-unconfirmed` to `reclaiming`. The next accepted restart's release phase and the manager shutdown sweep can therefore never both take an identity and never run concurrently, and shutdown additionally awaits `restartTasks` before its sweep so an in-flight reclamation is never raced. A quarantine record is deleted exactly once, only after an exact audit reports all three absences.
- **[R3] Task-set membership.** An abandoned launch promise is never added to `completionTasks`, `backgroundTasks`, `restartTasks`, or `stopTasks`. `restartTasks` holds only the public restart operation promise, which the restart's own deadline bounds at 66,000 ms or 81,000 ms, so shutdown's `restartTasks` await is finite. The delivered unbounded `while (completionTasks.size > 0 || backgroundTasks.size > 0)` drain therefore cannot observe a never-settling launch.

### Exact identity, attribution, and closure

- **Complete pre-restart identity** = the ownership tuple `(pid, processStartTime, port)` plus the entry's `ownerToken` and generation.
- **Distinct complete identity** for the replacement means the tuple differs from the pre-restart tuple in at least `(pid, processStartTime)`. The port may legitimately be reacquired once the prior listener is confirmed absent; the matrix records both tuples and asserts the pid/start-time difference rather than requiring a different port.
- Attribution remains process-group membership of the exact recorded root identity, and the delivered escape ceiling is recorded verbatim wherever the audit is documented or serialized: a descendant that leaves the owned group before the audit samples it is unattributable, and Ascend neither adopts nor terminates it.
- The gate requires all three absence booleans from one completed audit. An audit whose component observations did not all complete can never authorise a replacement.
- **[R3]** The gate additionally requires that no admission for that project remains unresolved and that every identity a resolved admission produced carries its own completed three-absence audit. Missing ownership is evidence of nothing; only a completed audit, or a launch rejection that never reported ownership, is evidence of absence.
- **[R4]** The gate further requires that every quarantine record for that project is `audited-absent`. A cleanup audit that completed but did not confirm all three absences is evidence of a residual, not of a release, and it is retained under its exact identity until a later audit confirms it. A confirming audit is reused rather than repeated, exactly as the delivered shutdown sweep reuses a confirming prior audit.

### Stale prior-generation settlements

| Stale settlement | Mechanism that isolates the successor | Asserted outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Prior release returns after the release deadline | abandoned by the sequencer, counted in `lateTerminationSettlements` | records nothing, mutates nothing, signals nothing, emits nothing |
| Prior process exit settles after the claim | guarded `running -> failed` compare-and-set on the exact entry and generation | loses silently; successor unchanged |
| Prior health observation settles after the claim | reuse recheck of the exact entry and generation | returns `runtime-restarting` or the winner's retained failure; zero mutations, zero events |
| Prior-generation proxy acquisition settles after the claim | `ownsSnapshot` false for the replaced snapshot | forwarding refused; no stale upstream reached |
| Prior-generation browser or proxy connection | released process, owned group, and listener are confirmed absent before any replacement exists | connection cannot communicate; no proxy broadcast or drain path added |
| Replacement launch completes after the restart deadline | post-launch abort check, mirroring the delivered `start` path | replacement terminated and audited, never installed; counted in `lateReplacementSettlements` |
| **[R2]** Replacement launch never settles and ignores cancellation, ownership already reported | restart-owned overall deadline armed by the trusted scheduler | caller settles at the bound as `replacement-failed` / `restart-deadline-exceeded`; ownership record retained, `replacementAuditState: 'unaudited-retained'`, absence never claimed |
| **[R3]** Replacement launch never settles and has not reported ownership | pending admission created before `launch`, plus the restart-owned overall deadline | caller settles at the bound as `replacement-failed` / `restart-deadline-exceeded` with `replacementAuditState: 'admission-unresolved'`; the admission stays `launch-pending`, the retained `failed` entry references it, no absence and no zero residual is claimed, and no later restart may pass its gate until it resolves |
| **[R3]** Replacement launch resolves long after the restart settled | detached `void launchPromise.then(...)` continuation, tracked by no awaited set | the exact identity is quarantined by admission plus identity tuple and immediately terminated and audited; `entries`, `ownership`, `cleanupOutcomes`, the successor, and the event stream are untouched; counted in `lateReplacementSettlements` |

| **[R4][R5]** Replacement attempt cleanup that does not confirm absence | restart-scoped wrapper, exact-identity keyed, aborting the phase; the launch then ends either at the next iteration's loop head (mid-loop collision) or by its own rethrow (readiness, health, early exit, last-attempt collision), and the manager's settlement reason selector supplies the retained category from `phaseSignal.reason` | the identity moves from `ownership` to `quarantinedOwnership` as `audited-unconfirmed` with its exact audit; the restart settles `replacement-failed` / `restart-replacement-unconfirmed`; no further port, process, or listener is created; `residualCount: null`, so no zero-residual or success claim exists |

| **[R2]** Prior release never settles and ignores cancellation | restart-owned release backstop armed by the trusted scheduler | caller settles at the release bound as `release-unconfirmed`; prior ownership record retained for the shutdown sweep |

### Concurrency contract

| Interleaving | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Restart vs restart, same project | second caller joins; one release, one launch, one event pair, identical results |
| Restart vs restart, different projects | fully independent entries, generations, operations, snapshots, and cleanup records |
| Restart vs `start()` | `start` on `restarting` throws `runtime-restarting`; the restart is unaffected |
| Restart vs `stop()` | `stop` on `restarting` returns `restart-in-progress`; restart on `stopping` returns `stop-in-progress` |
| Restart vs guarded exit/health transition | whichever installs first wins; the loser mutates, terminates, audits, and emits nothing |
| Restart vs proxy acquisition | proxy start receives `runtime-restarting`; a released snapshot fails `ownsSnapshot`; **[R2]** every proxy-reachable retained category (`stop-unconfirmed`, `runtime-stopping`, `restart-release-unconfirmed`, `restart-deadline-exceeded`, `runtime-restarting`) resolves to a published bounded `503` row instead of throwing |
| Restart vs manager shutdown | shutdown rejects new restarts with `manager-shutdown` and awaits every in-flight restart before auditing ownership |
| **[R3]** Restart vs an unresolved predecessor admission | the retry is accepted; its replacement gate waits for the admission inside the release bound and settles `release-unconfirmed` if it never resolves |
| **[R3]** Detached continuation vs resolving restart | the quarantine re-attempt is claimed by a synchronous compare-and-set, so exactly one of them terminates each identity |
| **[R4]** Restart vs an uncleared quarantine record for the same project | the retry is accepted and classified `quarantined-residual`; its release phase arms the 20,000 ms variant, claims and reclaims each record once, and its gate passes only when every record is `audited-absent` |
| **[R3]** Detached continuation vs manager shutdown | shutdown never awaits the launch promise, sweeps quarantine, reports unresolved admissions, and a post-shutdown materialisation is still terminated by the continuation |

### Event semantics and cardinality

- Accepted restart: exactly one `runtime.restart.requested` (`to: restarting`, public `Starting`).
- Settlement: exactly one terminal event — `runtime.restart.succeeded` (`to: running`, public `Running`) or `runtime.restart.failed` (`to: failed`, public `Failed`, carrying the bounded classification). **[R2]** The failure classification is exactly the retained failure category, so a pre-gate deadline classifies as `restart-release-unconfirmed` and a post-gate deadline classifies as `restart-deadline-exceeded`; **no** restart terminal event may ever classify as `manager-shutdown` while the manager is not shutting down.
- Pre-acceptance rejection: **zero** restart lifecycle events.
- Joined caller: zero additional events.
- Lost claim at settlement: zero events.
- A restart emits **no** `runtime.stop.*`, `runtime.start.*`, or `runtime.health.changed` event for its internal phases. This is why the restart consumes the termination sequencer and the launch primitive directly instead of calling `stop()` and `start()`.
- `PUBLIC_STATE_BY_LIFECYCLE_EVENT` gains `runtime.restart.requested: 'Starting'`, `runtime.restart.succeeded: 'Running'`, `runtime.restart.failed: 'Failed'`, and the existing agreement check rejects any mismatch.
- Route-level operational records `project.runtime.restart.rejected` and `project.runtime.restart.failed` are **not** lifecycle events, carry only the bounded route error category, and are never read, validated, or documented as NFR-015 events.

### Project Home interaction

- **Eligibility:** a card offers Restart when its reconciled report state is `Running` or `Failed`. `Stopped`, `Starting`, and any revision whose projection is unavailable offer no Restart control.
- **Lane:** a per-project restart-owner registry keyed by stable project ID, separate from the delivered single Home owner. Admission requires a successful list, `editing` mode, no open close dialog, no pending restart for that project, and no pending stop for that project. It does not claim, invalidate, or wait for the single Home owner.
- **Ownership:** each admitted restart holds its own `AbortController` and monotonic generation; a settlement applies only when the registry still holds that exact owner for that project and the project is still in the current list. Otherwise it is discarded without mutation.
- **Availability:** only the pending project's Restart control is disabled. Open, Close, and Stop controls key on their own pending state, so during a restart of P every other card's controls remain enabled and functional.
- **Accessibility:** the control is a `<button>` with `aria-label` `Restart <name> workbench` and `data-restart-project-id`; the card carries `aria-busy` while pending; polite announcements cover pending, success, failure, and unknown; a classified failure renders a `role="alert"` notice with the bounded client-owned notice text and a Retry restart action; the unknown outcome renders an explicit block with a Refresh runtime state action.
- **Focus:** on every settlement focus returns to that project's Restart control via `focusTarget: 'restart'` and `focusProjectId`.
- **Refresh:** a settled successful restart increments `restartSettlementVersion`, which triggers exactly one additional read-only runtime-state request for the current list revision. Failure and unknown trigger none. No timer, poll, stream, or client health probe is added.
- **Unknown outcome:** explicit, non-optimistic, no automatic retry, no second replacement, and accompanied by the recorded statement that this re-observation adds no backend reconciliation behaviour and does not claim BL-019.

**[R5] Residual-claim encoding — `residualCount` and `teardownResidualCount`.** Revision 4 made `residualCount` a required scalar, forbade a non-zero value everywhere, and simultaneously forbade a zero value on `unaudited-retained`, `admission-unresolved`, and `quarantined-unconfirmed` rows. That schema was uninhabitable: rows 47, 52, 62, and 63 had no legal value. It also conflated two different claims in one field — what the *operation* proved about the resources it touched, and what the *validation harness* proved about its own disposable fixtures at teardown. Revision 5 separates them and gives the first an explicit "no claim" value.

| Field | Type | Owner | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `residualCount` | `number \| null` | the restart operation | **Settlement residual knowledge.** The integer `0` means every resource in scope for this row's operation was observed absent by a completed exact audit. `null` means the operation withheld a residual claim because at least one resource in scope was never observed absent. No other value is legal. |
| `teardownResidualCount` | `number` | the validation harness | **Final validation-owned fixture cleanup.** The count of items classified `validation-owned-temporary` in this row's pre-declared inventory that were still present at final teardown. Always the integer `0`, on every row, including rows whose `residualCount` is `null`. |

`residualCount` is **`null` if and only if** the row holds at least one unresolved residual, which is this total, ordered predicate over values the row already records:

1. `replacementAuditState` is `unaudited-retained`, `admission-unresolved`, or `quarantined-unconfirmed` — the three states that exist precisely to name a resource the manager did not observe absent; **or**
2. `releaseMode` is `unconfirmed`, so the prior generation's identity was never confirmed absent and its ownership record is retained; **or**
3. `admission.resolution` is `unresolved`; **or**
4. `quarantine.recordCount` is non-zero with any `auditStates` member other than `audited-absent`.

Otherwise `residualCount` is the integer `0`. The validator rejects a `residualCount` that is a positive number, a negative number, a non-integer, `NaN`, a string, or absent; it rejects `null` on any row the predicate says is `0`; and it rejects `0` on any row the predicate says is `null`. Rule 1 discharges the requirement that those three replacement audit states are **always** `null`; rules 2-4 apply the identical principle to the release phase, the admission index, and the quarantine index, because forcing `0` onto a row whose prior identity was never audited absent would reintroduce exactly the false-absence defect this issue exists to prevent.

A scenario that executes a declared variant records the **most conservative** value across its executed paths: `null` when any executed path satisfies the predicate, `0` only when none does. Scenario 63 is therefore `null` — its non-confirming reclamation variant retains the record — while scenario 50 is `0`, because both of its paths audit every attempt absent.

`teardownResidualCount` is never evidence of a manager-side absence claim and may never be read as one. A quarantined identity, an unresolved admission, and a retained unaudited replacement are **not** counted in it; they are reported by the row's own `quarantine` and `admission` objects and, out of process, by the residual audit's `quarantinedIdentities` and `unresolvedAdmissions` arrays. In the deterministic matrix a non-confirming cleanup triple is a fixture **input** rather than an observation of a real port, so no host resource exists for it to leak; `teardownResidualCount: 0` and `residualCount: null` on the same row are both true and mean different things, which is exactly why they are two fields.

`admissionId` is an opaque correlation token like every other identity value in the schema; the serializer emits no raw PID, port, path, or authority for admissions or quarantined identities.

Serialization is deterministic: stable key order, fixed `bl018-<surface>-<scenario>` execution IDs, no wall-clock timestamps, no measured durations, no absolute paths, PIDs, ports, authorities, or tokens. Identity values are opaque correlation tokens, never raw PIDs or ports. Measured values live only in the disposable timing artifact.

The matrix test writes the disposable copy, asserts SHA-256 equality with the committed copy, re-reads the committed copy, reparses and revalidates it, and asserts that serializing the same validated matrix twice is byte-identical.

### Proof ceilings

- Manager-level race ordering, join behaviour, shutdown interleaving, deadline abandonment, and stale settlement are proven with the delivered injectable process, port, health, clock, and event dependencies, plus **[R2]** the injected recording `RuntimeDeadlineScheduler`. Deterministically producing a real host race with a fixed winner is neither repeatable nor bounded.
- **[R2]** The replacement allowance `runtimeReplacementBoundMs` is arithmetic over configured allowances, not a property the launch path enforces: `assertLaunchable`, `ports.acquire`, and `process.launch` are awaited without bounds of their own, and the per-attempt cleanup terminations are called without a caller signal so the restart cannot cancel them. The enforced guarantee is the caller-visible one — the restart settles within `runtimeRestartOverallBoundMs` because its overall deadline is armed by trusted synchronous work that no fallible primitive can delay.
- **[R2]** A cancellation-respecting launch primitive lets the restart prove zero replacement residuals through a completed three-absence audit (`replacementAuditState: 'audited-absent'`). A cancellation-violating launch primitive that already reported ownership does not: the restart records `replacementAuditState: 'unaudited-retained'`, retains the `onOwned` ownership record, claims no absence it did not observe, and leaves exactly one re-attempt to terminate and audit that identity. No evidence row, notice, or document may report a residual-free replacement that was never audited, and the validator rejects any that does. **[R5]** The `unaudited-retained` case records `residualCount: null`.
- **[R3] A launch that has not reported ownership is a strictly weaker position and is recorded as such.** The restart holds no handle, so it cannot terminate, cannot audit, and cannot claim absence; it records `replacementAuditState: 'admission-unresolved'` and retains the admission. The strongest honest claims available are: the caller settled inside its declared bound; no successor was installed; no later restart can pass its gate until the admission resolves; and any identity the launch later materialises is quarantined and terminated by the detached continuation. Zero residual is **not** among them; the row records `residualCount: null`, and **[R5]** M-7 rejects an `admission-unresolved` row whose `residualCount` is anything else.
- **[R4] What a blocked replacement can and cannot prove.** When a replacement attempt's cleanup audit completes but does not confirm all three absences, the manager holds an exact identity and an exact audit — strictly more than the `admission-unresolved` case and strictly less than `audited-absent`. The strongest honest claims are: the caller settled inside its declared bound; no successor was installed; no further port, process, or listener was created after the abort; the identity is retained under its exact quarantine key with its audit; and no restart for that project can launch a replacement until a later audit confirms it. Zero residual and success are not among them; the row records `residualCount: null`, and **[R5]** M-8 rejects a `quarantined-unconfirmed` row whose `residualCount` is anything else, or whose `outcome` is `restarted`. Note also what the audit's three booleans do and do not mean here: `listenerAbsent` is a probe of the attempted port, not of an owned listener, so a non-confirming triple is treated as a residual regardless of which boolean failed — the conservative reading, matching the delivered confirmed-release rule, and never the reading that would let a second generation start.
- **[R4] The delivered `start()` path leaks a collided attempt's ownership record, and BL-018 records that rather than repairing it.** `start` passes `registerOwnership` and `recordCleanup` straight to `launchReadyRuntime`, so a launch that survives a port collision registers each attempt and deletes none; the project-keyed cleanup index keeps only the last audit. BL-018 repairs this for the restart path, where the wrappers it must install anyway make the fix exact and free, and does not change `start`'s callbacks, because that would alter delivered BL-010 and BL-017 behaviour, evidence, and source guards for a defect this issue did not introduce and cannot observe from its own surfaces. The consequence is bounded and stated: a *pre-restart* generation created by an Open flow that collided may carry a stale record; the restart releases the entry's live handle and does not terminate that record; the manager shutdown sweep still terminates and audits every remaining ownership record; and BL-018's one-record and zero-residual claims are scoped to generations a restart created plus the prior live identity it audited, which is exactly what the deterministic matrix constructs and asserts. The residual-audit CLI reports any record it cannot prove absent rather than converting it into a zero.
- **[R2]** The delivered selected-stop caller backstop still races `processDependencies.sleep`. BL-018 neither repairs nor inherits that ceiling, and no BL-018 surface may describe it as trusted-scheduler armed.
- **[R2]** The workbench navigation shell keeps its 15,000 ms document timeout. It is never binding during `restarting`, because a navigation arriving then is refused immediately with the bounded `503`. The delivered case in which a shell navigation times out while a slow `start` is still running — worst case `3 x (15,000 + 5,000)` for the launch path — is pre-existing, unchanged by this issue, and is not a restart claim. Its outcome for the user is explicitly the shell's retryable timeout error, not a restart result.
- Deterministic fakes are **not** acceptable evidence for AC-1's real release-and-replace episode, AC-4's stale-connection behaviour, or AC-17's residual audit. Those are proven by the designated real-host episode: one real code-server generation with a real loopback listener and one real established connection, a real restart, the real post-release exact-identity closure, and a real ready replacement.
- The designated episode and the independent residual audit are deliberately separate. The episode is self-reported by the test process; the residual audit is a separate out-of-process CLI that re-probes the recorded prior root identity, every recorded owned-group member identity, the owned process group, the prior listener, and the retained stale connection, so the episode is not self-certifying.
- **[R3] What each side of that split can and cannot prove about late closure.** The designated episode runs *inside* the API process that owns the manager. It can hold real browser and proxy connections, drive a real restart, and read the manager's own admission and quarantine indexes, but it cannot independently prove that a late-materialising process or listener closed, because the only observer it has is the same process that is being asked to prove the claim, and waiting for an unbounded launch would violate AC-20. The independent residual audit is the authority for late process, owned-group, and listener closure, because it re-probes recorded exact identities from a separate process after the episode ended. Its ceiling is that it can only probe identities that were **recorded**: an admission that never materialised has no identity to probe, so the audit reports it as an `unresolved-admission` entry rather than as a proven absence, and no artifact converts that into a zero-residual claim.
- **[R3] No real episode may finish while it still knows about an unaudited resource.** The designated episode MUST, before it writes its artifact and returns, have every identity it recorded — the prior root identity, every recorded owned-group member, the replacement, and any quarantined late identity — audited absent, or it MUST fail. It therefore never creates a `launch-pending` admission it leaves behind, and the never-settling-launch, late-materialisation, and unresolved-shutdown cases are proven **only** in the deterministic matrix, where the launch primitive is injected and the deadline is fired synchronously. That division is stated in the episode artifact, in the residual-audit artifact, and in `docs/project-runtime.md`.
- Descendant attribution is bounded by owned process-group membership, and the delivered escape ceiling is recorded verbatim in the ADR, the core-component, the evidence schema, the designated episode, the residual audit, and the shipped documentation.
- Restart claims no editor or terminal session continuity across replacement, and no evidence row, notice, or document implies it.
- BL-019 restart-after-API-process-restart reconciliation is out of scope and is never treated as satisfied by any row. A stopped project's Open flow is never recorded as a Restart success.
